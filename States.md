# States

Altered or atypical phenomenal states the community distinguishes and discusses. Each has its own dynamics, training implications, and terminology boundaries. None is treated as purely pathological in community usage.

----

# Dissociation

Disconnection between mental awareness and sensory / physical reality — feeling flat, unreal, or observing oneself from outside the body.

Community usage is **non-clinical**: a spectrum state from mild derealization to full depersonalization. Distinct from Dissociative Identity Disorder (DID).

## Phenomenology

• Depth perception attenuated; world feels "plastic," "fake," or lucid-dream-like.

• Self feels decoupled from bodily gravity and spatial presence.

• Correlated with Aphantasia and high reliance on Verbal Thinking + Conceptual Thinking without Sensory Thinking.

## Community mechanism model

Reduced bandwidth of sensory processing applied to external reality. The mind models the world conceptually without _feeling_ it through the senses. Shares neural correlates with trauma-dissociation and dreaming (PFC–limbic dysregulation) but differs in etiology.

## Countermeasures

• Active Sensory Thinking applied to perception — textures, temperatures, kinesthetic awareness.

• 3D Sight practice restores the felt depth of the environment.

• Reducing overuse of analogue / conceptual thinking.

• Mindfulness of body in space.

Inverse correlation is commonly reported: as sensory imagination capacity increases, dissociation decreases.

# Tulpa

A constructed sentient-feeling entity within one mind — a self-aware companion developed through sustained attention, personality definition, and habitual interaction.

## Community framing

• **Not** a separate consciousness or possession; framed as a conscious _identity shift_ within a single mind.

• Functionally an imaginary friend given _automacity_ (autonomous response) via long practice.

• **Not** equivalent to DID (trauma-based, non-interactive, unconscious) or psychotic hallucination.

• Sometimes compared to perceptual shifts with optical illusions — same substrate, viewed from a different identity.

## Development arc

1. **Definition** - specify personality traits, voice, values.
2. **Forcing** - dedicated interaction time (narration, conversation).
3. **Automation** (~6+ months typical) - tulpa responds to circumstances without prompting.
4. **Imposition** - optional advanced step; the tulpa appears to the practitioner as present in the room.
5. **Switching** - perspective shift to think _as_ the tulpa.

## Mechanism notes

• Relies on diversified Inner Monologue — distinct voice patterns per identity.

• Benefits from Prophantasia for visual tangibility and Imposition for scene integration.

• Shares mechanisms with Dissociation but is intentional and non-pathological.

• Memory sharing between host and tulpa varies across practitioners.

# Tetris Effect

Involuntary intrusion of visual, behavioral, and thought patterns following prolonged, intensive exposure to a repetitive stimulus. Named for post-gaming Tetris imagery; generalizes to any sustained visual focus (Factorio belts, chess positions, mandalas, territorial maps).

## Manifestations

• Automatic mental replay of experienced sequences.

• Behavioral mimicry — trying real-world interactions learned in-game.

• Modified perception of unrelated stimuli.

• Appears in closed-eye waking states, hypnagogic transitions, and dreams.

• Occupies a distinct perceptual screen — low-contrast, motion-based, behind the eyelids.

## Relations

• Shares space and mechanism with Hypnagogia.

• Can coexist with Prophantasia; sometimes serves as an entry point to voluntary visualization.

• Meditation on Tetris-effect imagery, with attempts to modify it, is reported to accelerate the transition to voluntary Autogogia.

• Distinct from rumination and intrusive thoughts — involves genuine visual content, not just cognitive replay.

• Chess study reportedly produces unusually vivid and persistent effects lasting days.

## Training use

Deliberate induction via high-engagement, visually-complex, repetitive activity (gaming, pattern art, N-back) can be used as scaffolding for practitioners who struggle to initiate voluntary imagery.
